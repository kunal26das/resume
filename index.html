Mobile engineer, six years in, shipping production Kotlin, Swift and TypeScript into the same repository. I build the layer underneath mobile products — the Gradle build, the React Native runtime, cold‑start instrumentation, the crash programme, the release train — and I work across all three stacks because that layer does not respect platform boundaries.

At Licious, India’s largest D2C fresh‑meat brand, I am the single largest contributor to both the Android and the iOS codebases, on a team of sixty‑three engineers. I was not hired for iOS. The pattern is older than this job: I have created the module graph of four production codebases, taken one of them off Java entirely, and built two companies’ chat products single‑handed. On contract at Wish I merged the company’s iOS and Android repositories into one and built the Kotlin Multiplatform layer between them, shipping a Compose Multiplatform surface to both app stores from a single source.

A good share of that is subtraction. Six third‑party SDKs out of the shipping app, a homegrown crash handler that was quietly hiding crashes from reporting, an entire monitoring vendor added and taken out again without touching a line of product code. Fewer moving parts, and fewer places for the app to fail without anyone noticing.
